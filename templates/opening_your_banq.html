Opening Your Banq

First you find out what you own. Then you decide how you want to own it. You only need to do this once.

Step One
1. Divide your wardrobe

Split your wardrobe into clear sections that work for you. For example: Tops, trousers, jumpers, coats, shoes, dresses, sportswear, accessories.

Why sectionsThe reset stays manageable and finishable. Do one category, or one shelf, per weekend or per day. If it's all mixed together, work by physical space instead: one shelf, rail, or drawer at a time.
2. Try everything on and sort

Take everything out and go through it properly. As you go, sort each piece into one of four piles.

Keeplog these on the Wardrobe page in step 3.
Sellset these aside for Part Two.
Donatebag them and drop them off. They leave your system here, which is the point.
Not surelog these too, and mark resale willingness as "maybe sell." Once the whole section is logged, seeing it together makes the call much easier.
3. Lodge what you are keeping

Before putting anything away, open the Wardrobe page and log each item in your keep pile, filling in every column.

Whythis is what builds your wardrobe data. Every total, filter and pattern the system shows you comes from here.
4. Use the system to reassess

Once a section is logged, pause. Go back to the Wardrobe page and look at what you actually own.

Askdo I have too many of one type? Is there anything I could sell instead?
Thenmove items between your piles if needed.
Whythis is where the system starts guiding your decisions, not just storing them.
5. Organise + reset your space

Reset the space itself before anything goes back.

→ clean it first
→ return only what you are keeping
→ store seasonal items separately

Step Two
6. Prepare and list your items

Open the Sales page and add every item from your sell pile. Then set the stage each one is at, and update it as it moves towards sold.

→ wash anything that needs it, iron if needed
→ take clear photos: plain background, front and back, plus anything a buyer would want to see (a flaw, the label, the fit)

The stagesto prep, washing, photographed, listed, sold, posted, money received.
Tiptake a painting off the wall and use the nail to hang each piece while you photograph it.
Whyonce everything is on the Sales page, it stays organised as you sell.
7. Upload gradually

Do not upload everything at once. List a few items at a time, using spare moments like a commute or an evening.

Whythis keeps it manageable, and spacing listings out often improves how they perform.
8. Postage + packaging

I use Vinted. It is simpler and more cost-effective than Depop, especially on shipping. If a better option appears, use that though of course.

When posting on Vinted, select the digital label instead of printing, and screenshot it on the day you post.

Whythis avoids printing costs and reduces paper.
Materialsuse what you already have: small boxes, shoe boxes, delivery packaging, shopping bags. You only need tape and scissors.
Low cost optionbin bags.
If using a bin bagtape a piece of paper to the front so the shipping label sticks properly.
Label each packagewrite a small note on each package right after you've sealed it so you can match it at the post office and avoid sending the wrong item.
9. When it sells, record it

When an item sells, mark it Sold on the Sales page and log what you got for it.

Watchyour revenue builds on the Progress page as you go.
Why this mattersthe sale now lives in your system, not just in the app. You always know what you have made, and what is still waiting to sell.
Final note

You only need to do this once. After that, everything is simple.

Add new pieces to your Wardrobe tab as you get them, and move items to Sales when you are ready. Everything is already there, so listing is quick and easy.

Your entire wardrobe, organised, trackable, and ready to use or sell.
